Dating Again After Heartbreak: When You're Ready and How to Start

EL
ExLane Editorial · 8 min read · Updated Sept 2026
Reviewed against published relationship-psychology research; not medical advice.

There is no correct number of months. There is a set of signals. Here they are, plus how to re-enter without dragging your ex along.

The readiness checklist

  • You can tell the breakup story in under two minutes without heat.
  • You're not checking their profile (or you check and feel nothing).
  • You're dating to add to a life, not to fill a hole.
  • You know your three non-negotiables and your two dealbreakers.
  • A quiet weekend alone is fine. Not great. Fine.
  • You'd be okay if the first date went nowhere.

Four or more: go. Two or three: date lightly, no exclusivity. Fewer: keep working the plan; you're close.

On rebounds

The evidence is mixed. Some studies find people who start new relationships sooner report higher wellbeing and confidence; others find suppressed grief resurfaces later. The difference seems to be honesty: a rebound where you're clear with yourself and the other person ("I'm recently out of something; I'm taking it slow") is different from using someone as anaesthetic.

Profiles that don't mention your ex

Delete anything defined by absence: "not looking for drama", "no games", "finally free". Those are about them. Write about what you do on a Tuesday, what you're learning, what you'd want to show someone in your city. Photos: at least one where you're doing something, one clear face, none cropped from couple shots.

First-date rules for a healed heart

  1. Don't tell the breakup story on date one. If asked, one sentence.
  2. Notice comparisons and name them privately. "That's a comparison." Then return to the person in front of you.
  3. Keep it short. Ninety minutes, daytime or early evening, easy exit.
  4. Score the date on how you felt about yourself, not on whether they liked you.
  5. No texting your ex afterwards to "process". Text a friend.

If it's too soon

You'll know: you feel worse after dates, not better; you're scanning for your ex's traits; you cry in the bathroom. Nothing is lost. Pause, go back to the tools, and try again in a month. Readiness is not a deadline.
