How to Get Over an Ex: The 7-Stage Recovery Roadmap

EL
ExLane Editorial · 11 min read · Updated Sept 2026
Reviewed against published relationship-psychology research; not medical advice.

Grief after a breakup is not linear, but it is patterned. Knowing the pattern turns "why do I still feel like this?" into "this is stage four, and here is what stage four needs."

How long does it take?

Surveys put the average at about 3 months for a typical relationship and 12–18 months after a long-term partnership or divorce. The spread is enormous, and the biggest predictors are not relationship length. They are: whether you're still in contact, whether the breakup was ambiguous, and whether your life has other sources of identity and connection.

Shock and disbelief

Days 1–7. Numbness, autopilot, replaying the last conversation. Your only task is basic maintenance: eat, sleep, tell two people, and set no-contact.

Withdrawal

Weeks 1–3. Physical symptoms: chest tightness, insomnia, appetite changes. This is the stage most people mistake for proof that they should get back together. It is chemistry, not evidence. Use the tools; don't make decisions.

Bargaining and detective work

Weeks 2–6. Checking profiles, analysing texts, constructing scenarios where it works out. The stuck point: intermittent contact. The move: full no-contact plus a "reasons list" you re-read when the story starts rewriting itself.

Anger

Weeks 4–10. Often a relief after sadness. Useful if it powers boundaries; corrosive if it becomes a hobby. Channel it into the physical (workouts, cleaning, building something), not into contact.

Sadness proper

Months 2–4. Quieter, deeper, less dramatic. This is the actual grieving. Let it be there. Journal, talk, cry in the car. Anti-depressant behaviours (movement, sunlight, people, sleep) matter most here.

Rebuilding

Months 3–6. Interest returns. New routines, people, and a sense of self that doesn't reference them. The Glow-Up Plan lives here.

Integration

Month 6 onward. They become a chapter, not the plot. You can think of them without a body response. Dating is a choice, not a cure.

Where people get stuck

  • Stage 3 forever: caused by contact and ambiguity. Fix with no-contact and a decision you make yourself, not one you wait for.
  • Skipping stage 5: numbing with alcohol, immediate dating, or overwork. The sadness waits for you. Six months later it lands in a new relationship.
  • Stage 4 as identity: the bitter ex. Anger is a tool, not a personality.

What actually speeds it up

Studies on breakup recovery consistently point to the same levers: no contact, reflective writing (expressive journaling about the breakup for 15–20 minutes, several times a week), social connection (not advice; presence), physical movement, and self-concept rebuilding: deliberately doing things that are yours. Rebound dating shows mixed results: it helps confidence in some studies and delays grief in others.
