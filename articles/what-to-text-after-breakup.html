What to Text After a Breakup (and What Never to Send)

EL
ExLane Editorial · 6 min read · Updated Sept 2026
Reviewed against published relationship-psychology research; not medical advice.

Most post-breakup texting is unnecessary. The small amount that is necessary should be short, factual, and final. Scripts below. Copy, adapt, send once.

Necessary messages

Returning items

"I've put your things in a bag. I'll leave it with [friend/at the front desk] on [day]. No need to reply."

Shared bills or accounts

"I've cancelled the [Netflix/lease renewal/phone plan] as of [date]. You'll need to set up your own. I'll transfer your half of the deposit by [date]."

Setting the boundary

"I'm going to stop texting for a while so I can actually move on. It's not a game and I don't need a reply. Take care."

If they keep messaging

"I meant what I said about space. I'm not going to reply for now. Please respect that."

Then don't reply. One boundary message, one reminder, then mute or block.

Messages that always backfire

  • The closure request. "Can you just tell me why?" They already told you, or they can't. Either way the answer won't close anything. Closure is something you give yourself.
  • The essay. Anything over four lines. They will skim it, and you will refresh for hours.
  • The "I'm doing great." They know why you sent it.
  • The drunk text. Use the Urge SOS. Ten minutes and a glass of water beats a week of regret.
  • The anniversary / birthday text. It reopens the file. If you must, send it to a friend instead and let them not-forward it.
  • The reply to their breadcrumb. "Hey stranger", a story reaction, a meme. Breadcrumbs check whether the door is open. Keep it closed.

The unsent message tool

The journal has a prompt for exactly this: write the text you want to send, then what you'd tell a friend who wanted to send it. Reading the two side by side ends most urges.
